## Project title

Myth vs Fact: Living Donation for New Yorkers

## Here's my idea (describe the project)

Right now, patients are waiting for a living kidney donor, a living liver donor or a bone marrow match. One of them is 3-year-old William, who has end-stage kidney disease, needs a type O living kidney donor, and spends 10 hours on dialysis every night. Many people could help, but they have never been asked, or they believe myths about what being a living donor means.

Save One Person is a small New York nonprofit that uses media to share these patients' stories. We've worked with PIX11, NY1, FOX 5, 1010 WINS and the NYC Mayor's Office. Our motto: "One story can reach millions. One introduction can save a life."

With $1,000 we'll turn our new "Myth vs Fact: Living Kidney Donation" page into a short, shareable video series and a printable one-page guide for New Yorkers. Each short video tackles one common myth in plain language and points viewers to the real patient stories on our site and to our free donor sign-up list, which never commits anyone to donate. We'll share the series on social media and in our weekly Save One Alert, offer it to our NYC media contacts, and hand out the printed guides at community events. ## A little about me / us

I'm Lauren Finkelstein, founder and CEO of Save One Person Inc., a New York 501(c)(3). We are "a miracle-making force, harnessing the power of media to bring hope and healing." We publish the stories of patients who urgently need a living donor or match (73 are on our site right now), send a weekly Save One Alert to the public and to journalists, and make short "Health Hacks" videos on disease prevention. We've published 20 success stories. Bonnie watched a Save One Alert on PIX11, entered a kidney swap program, and the chain saved 8 lives. Tami read a Save One Alert and saved John's life. Sara saw a man's story on TV and saved his life. Marisa gave a kidney to a stranger. We've helped hundreds of patients, and we've also been featured on WWOR Radio, eHealth Radio, Our Town and the Tony Robbins Podcast.

## How does this project directly benefit New York City and New Yorkers?

New Yorkers who need a living organ donor or a bone marrow match depend on other New Yorkers learning that they can help: neighbors, coworkers, fellow parents, strangers on the subway. This project gives the city free, plain-language answers to the questions that stop people from taking a first step. It sends viewers straight to real New York patients who are waiting. All medical evaluation is done by transplant centers; our job is to start the conversation. One New Yorker who watches a 60-second video and signs up to learn more could save a neighbor's life.
