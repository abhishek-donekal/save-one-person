## Request title

Save One Person: Media Storytelling to Connect Transplant Patients with Living Donors and Matches

## Request summary (short description)

Save One Person is a New York 501(c)(3) that uses television, radio, podcasts, magazines and social media to share the stories of patients who need a living kidney donor, a living liver donor, a bone marrow match or another medical miracle. We seek support for our core storytelling program: publishing patient stories, distributing our weekly Save One Alert to the public and to journalists, and educating the public about living donation, so that more people learn they can help and take the first step. As we say: "One story can reach millions. One introduction can save a life."

## Organization background and mission

Save One Person Inc. is "a miracle-making force, harnessing the power of media to bring hope and healing." Founded and led by Lauren Finkelstein, the organization tells the stories of people facing life-threatening illness who need a living organ donor, a bone marrow match or another medical miracle, and puts those stories in front of the people most likely to help.

We have worked with media outlets and partners including PIX11, NY1, FOX 5, 1010 WINS, WWOR Radio, eHealth Radio, Our Town, the Tony Robbins Podcast and the NYC Mayor's Office. Our website currently lists 73 patients in its Urgent Requests section. We have published 20 success stories, and our About page notes that we have helped hundreds of patients.

## Statement of need

Many patients with kidney failure, liver failure or blood cancers can be helped by a living organ donor or a bone marrow match. The hardest part is often finding that person. Patients and families are usually not media professionals, and many do not know how to ask for help publicly, or even that a stranger, coworker or friend could be a donor.

At the same time, most members of the public have never been asked. Many hold common misconceptions about living donation, and few know where to start. When a real patient's story reaches the right person, that person can act.

Save One Person closes this gap. We turn a patient's need into a clear, compassionate story and deliver it through trusted media outlets and our own channels. We pair it with simple education so viewers know what living donation involves and how to take the first step with a transplant center.

## Program description (what the funding supports)

1. **Urgent Requests: patient story publishing.** We write and publish the stories of patients who need a living kidney donor, living liver donor, bone marrow match or other medical miracle. For example, William Fritts, age 3, has end-stage kidney disease and needs a living kidney donor with blood type O; he is on dialysis every night. Each story explains the patient's need and how a potential donor can start the process. Our site currently lists 73 urgent requests.
2. **Save One Alert: weekly email and media alerts.** Every week we send a Save One Alert featuring patients in need to our email list. We also send media alerts to journalists so TV, radio, print and podcast outlets can share the stories with much larger audiences.
3. **Living kidney donor sign-up list.** People who want to help can join our living kidney donor list. We make clear that signing up never commits anyone to donate. It is a low-pressure first step that keeps interested people connected.
4. **Donor education.** Our blog and new "Myth vs Fact: Living Kidney Donation" page answer common questions and correct misconceptions in plain language. This helps people make informed decisions and know what to ask a transplant team.
5. **Health Hacks videos.** Short weekly segments on disease prevention and healthier living. They build an audience and keep people engaged between urgent appeals.

All medical decisions, donor evaluations and matching are handled by transplant centers and registries. Save One Person's role is awareness, storytelling and connection.

## Population served

Patients in need of a living kidney donor, living liver donor, bone marrow match or other life-saving help, and their families, together with the general public and potential donors reached through media and our own channels. We are based in New York, and our stories reach audiences across the New York media market and beyond through national podcasts and social media.
